    Bibliography¶

    All works listed are real publications; entries flagged in the text as resting on thinner evidence say so there. Editions and translations of primary sources are grouped after the general works.

    Standard General Works¶

    • Berggren, J. L. Episodes in the Mathematics of Medieval Islam. Springer, 1986; 2nd ed. 2016. The standard English introduction, organized by topic with worked mathematics.
    • Katz, Victor J. A History of Mathematics: An Introduction. 3rd ed., Addison-Wesley, 2009. Chapter-length, mathematically detailed treatment of Islamic mathematics in world context.
    • Katz, Victor J. (ed.). The Mathematics of Egypt, Mesopotamia, China, India, and Islam: A Sourcebook. Princeton University Press, 2007. Translated primary sources; the Islam chapter (Berggren) is the best single anthology in English.
    • Rashed, Roshdi. The Development of Arabic Mathematics: Between Arithmetic and Algebra. Kluwer, 1994. The arithmetization-of-algebra thesis: al-Karaji, al-Samawal, al-Farisi.
    • Rashed, Roshdi (ed.). Encyclopedia of the History of Arabic Science. 3 vols. Routledge, 1996.
    • Dictionary of Scientific Biography (DSB), Scribner's, 1970–1980, with the New DSB (2008). The scholar-by-scholar articles cited throughout ("DSB").
    • Rosenfeld, B. A. & Ihsanoglu, E. Mathematicians, Astronomers and Other Scholars of Islamic Civilization and Their Works (7th–19th c.). IRCICA, Istanbul, 2003. The bio-bibliographical census.
    • Van Brummelen, Glen. The Mathematics of the Heavens and the Earth: The Early History of Trigonometry. Princeton University Press, 2009.
    • Saliba, George. Islamic Science and the Making of the European Renaissance. MIT Press, 2007.
    • King, David A. In Synchrony with the Heavens: Studies in Astronomical Timekeeping and Instrumentation in Medieval Islamic Civilization. 2 vols. Brill, 2004–2005.
    • Kennedy, E. S. "A Survey of Islamic Astronomical Tables." Transactions of the American Philosophical Society 46.2 (1956).
    • Sayili, Aydin. The Observatory in Islam. Turkish Historical Society, 1960.
    • Gutas, Dimitri. Greek Thought, Arabic Culture: The Graeco-Arabic Translation Movement in Baghdad. Routledge, 1998.
    • Rosenfeld, B. A. A History of Non-Euclidean Geometry. Springer, 1988.
    • Smith, A. Mark. From Sight to Light: The Passage from Ancient to Modern Optics. University of Chicago Press, 2015.
    • Boyer, Carl B. The Rainbow: From Myth to Mathematics. 1959; reprint Princeton, 1987.
    • Kunitzsch, Paul. The Arabs and the Stars. Variorum, 1989. (Star names and transmission.)
    • Djebbar, Ahmed. Une histoire de la science arabe. Seuil, 2001; and his technical studies of Maghrebi mathematics.
    • Lamrabet, Driss. Introduction à l'histoire des mathématiques maghrébines. Rabat, 1994; 2nd ed. 2014.
    • Hogendijk, Jan P. & Sabra, A. I. (eds.). The Enterprise of Science in Islam: New Perspectives. MIT Press, 2003.

    Reliable Online Resources¶

    • MacTutor History of Mathematics Archive (University of St Andrews): scholar biographies with references. https://mathshistory.st-andrews.ac.uk/

    Verification advice

    For classroom or publication use, verify dates and attributions against DSB/New DSB or Rosenfeld & Ihsanoglu, which are the census-grade references; popular websites frequently repeat inflated claims this reference has tried to flag (Ibn Hamza and logarithms, the letter x, "Copernicus copied" without qualification).
