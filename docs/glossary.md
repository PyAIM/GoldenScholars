# Glossary

**al-jabr** — "Restoration/completion": moving a subtracted quantity to the other side of an equation as an added one. Half of the name of al-Khwarizmi's method; source of the word *algebra*.

**al-muqabala** — "Balancing/confrontation": cancelling equal terms from both sides of an equation. The other half of the method's name.

**algorism** — Medieval Latin term for written computation with Hindu-Arabic numerals, from *Algorismi* (al-Khwarizmi). Ancestor of *algorithm*.

**astrolabe** — Analog computer for astronomical problems, based on stereographic projection of the celestial sphere onto a plane; the standard scientific instrument of the era. The *safiha* (saphaea) of al-Zarqali is a universal (any-latitude) variant; Sharaf al-Din al-Tusi's linear astrolabe compresses one onto a rod.

**dust board (takht)** — Erasable computing surface on which Indian-reckoning algorithms were first performed; al-Uqlidisi advertised adapting them to ink and paper.

**equant** — Ptolemy's device: a point about which a planet's epicycle center moves with uniform *angular* speed, violating uniform circular motion and provoking the Islamic critical tradition from Ibn al-Haytham to the Maragha school.

**girih** — The strapwork pattern family of Islamic geometric ornament; connected to the constructional geometry of Abu al-Wafa's artisan handbook and, in modern analysis, to quasi-periodic tilings.

**hisab** — Practical calculation/arithmetic; *hisab al-hind* = Indian (decimal positional) reckoning.

**ilm al-fara'id** — The law/science of inheritance shares; a driver of exact fraction arithmetic and the application field of half of al-Khwarizmi's Algebra.

**ilm al-miqat** — The science of astronomical timekeeping (prayer times etc.); profession of the *muwaqqit*.

**jayb** — Arabic for sine (see [Etymology](legacy/etymology.md) for the mistranslation to *sinus*).

**madrasa** — Endowed teaching college; the institutional home of later mathematical instruction (Samarkand's being the most scientifically ambitious).

**mal** — "Wealth/property": the technical term for the square of the unknown in Arabic algebra (the unknown itself being *shay*, "thing," or *jidhr*, "root").

**Maragha school** — The 13th-century observatory group (Tusi, Urdi, Shirazi, al-Maghribi) and their successors (Ibn al-Shatir) who rebuilt planetary geometry from uniform circular motions.

**muwaqqit** — Mosque-employed astronomer/timekeeper (Ibn al-Shatir and al-Khalili held the post in Damascus).

**qibla** — The direction of Mecca; determining it from arbitrary coordinates is a central spherical-trigonometry problem of the tradition.

**rub al-mujayyab** — The sine quadrant: a quarter-circle instrument with a trigonometric grid, used to solve triangles graphically; an analog trig calculator.

**shadow functions** — The tangent and cotangent in their original form: shadow lengths of a standard gnomon (vertical or horizontal). *Zill* = shadow.

**shay** — "Thing": the unknown, x, of Arabic algebra.

**Tusi couple** — Two-circle rolling mechanism (radii in ratio 1:2) converting circular motions into straight-line oscillation; introduced by Nasir al-Din al-Tusi (1247/1261 works), used to replace the equant; reappears in Copernicus.

**Urdi's lemma** — Geometric equivalence (al-Urdi, before 1259) letting an eccentric-with-equant be replaced by concentric circles plus an epicyclet; a second core tool of Maragha planetary theory, also present in Copernicus's models.

**zij** — Astronomical handbook: theory reduced to tables plus instructions. The tradition's characteristic mathematical-astronomical publication (200+ known); ancestor, through the Toledan and Alfonsine tables, of the modern ephemeris.
